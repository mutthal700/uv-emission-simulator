## Q2 — CO₂ generation and occupancy inversion

### 1. Persily and de Jonge

**Document:** A. Persily and L. de Jonge, *Carbon dioxide generation rates for building occupants*  
**Publication/year:** *Indoor Air* 27 (2017), 868–879  
**DOI:** 10.1111/ina.12383  
**Locators:** §2.2, Equations (2)–(4); §4, Equations (8)–(11); Tables 1 and 4  
**Primary full text:** [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=920870)

#### Legacy body-surface-area formulation — §2.2, Equations (3)–(4)

\[
A_D=0.202H^{0.725}W^{0.425}
\]

\[
\dot V_{\mathrm{CO_2}}
=\frac{0.00276A_DMRQ}{0.23RQ+0.77}
\]

Here, \(A_D\) is DuBois surface area, \(M\) is activity level in met and \(RQ\) is respiratory quotient.

#### Updated BMR formulation — §4, Equations (8)–(11)

For these equations, BMR is in MJ/day and the reported output is L/s per person.

At 273 K and 101 kPa:

\[
\dot V_{\mathrm{CO_2}}
=RQ\,BMR\,M\,(0.000569)
\]

For the paper's calculation case of \(RQ=0.85\):

\[
\dot V_{\mathrm{CO_2}}
=BMR\,M\,(0.000484)
\]

At temperature \(T\) in kelvin and pressure \(P\) in kPa:

\[
\dot V_{\mathrm{CO_2}}
=RQ\,BMR\,M\,\frac{T}{P}(0.000211)
\]

For \(RQ=0.85\):

\[
\dot V_{\mathrm{CO_2}}
=BMR\,M\,\frac{T}{P}(0.000179)
\]

### 3. ICU-specific measured VCO₂ and REE evidence

#### Sedated, mechanically ventilated patients

**Document:** Altunalan et al., *Metabolic and hemodynamic responses to early passive range of motion in sedated critically ill adults*  
**Publication/year:** *BMC Anesthesiology* 26, Article 89 (2026)  
**Locator:** Results, “Metabolic response (VCO₂ and VO₂),” Table 2  
**Primary article:** [Springer Nature](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12871-025-03565-2)

The study included 23 invasively mechanically ventilated adults with RASS scores from −2 to −4. Source-reported VCO₂ means were:

| Measurement phase | VCO₂ reported by source |
|---|---:|
| Baseline | 188.362 mL/min |
| Sixth minute of passive range-of-motion intervention | 203.000 mL/min |
| Early recovery/rest | 189.812 mL/min |

These are study-cohort measurements, not a universal ICU-patient generation rate.

#### Broader mechanically ventilated ICU cohorts

**Document:** Kagan et al., *Validation of carbon dioxide production (VCO₂) as a tool to calculate resting energy expenditure (REE) in mechanically ventilated critically ill patients: a retrospective observational study*  
**Publication/year:** *Critical Care* 22, Article 186 (2018)  
**Locator:** Results  
**Primary article:** [Springer Nature](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13054-018-2108-8)

- Population: 80 invasively ventilated ICU patients; 497 measurements.
- Source-reported mean six-hour-block VCO₂: **244.5 ± 85.9 mL/min**.
- Source-reported mean indirect-calorimetry REE: **2059.5 ± 491.7 kcal/day**.
- Source-reported mean RQ: **0.75 ± 0.07**.
- The study states that sedation was not taken into account; it is not a sedated-only cohort.

**Document:** Rousing et al., *Energy expenditure in critically ill patients estimated by population-based equations, indirect calorimetry and CO₂-based indirect calorimetry*  
**Publication/year:** *Annals of Intensive Care* 6, Article 16 (2016)  
**Locator:** Results, “Comparing estimates of energy expenditure”  
**Primary article:** [Springer Nature](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13613-016-0118-8)

- Population: 18 intubated and mechanically ventilated ICU patients.
- Source-reported mean VCO₂: **273 ± 63 mL/min**.
- Source-reported mean VO₂: **343 ± 77 mL/min**.
- Source-reported mean RQ: **0.81**.
- Source-reported mean energy expenditure: **2347 kcal/day**.
- Sedation was recorded, but the paper does not define the cohort as uniformly sedated.

No L/s values derived from the above mL/min measurements are treated as reported source data in this audit.

### 4. Penn State REE equations

**Document:** Murray et al., *Comparison of predictive equations and indirect calorimetry in critical care: Does the accuracy differ by body mass index classification?*  
**Publication/year:** *Nutrition in Clinical Practice* (2023)  
**Locator:** Table 1  
**Primary article:** [Wiley/ASPEN](https://aspenjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ncp.11017)

Penn State 2003b, for the population category specified in Table 1:

\[
REE=0.96(MSJ)+31V_e+167T_{max}-6212
\]

Penn State 2010, for the older-obese population category specified in Table 1:

\[
REE=0.71(MSJ)+64V_e+85T_{max}-3085
\]

In the table, \(V_e\) is minute ventilation in L/min and \(T_{max}\) is maximum temperature in °C within 24 hours of the indirect-calorimetry study.

**Supporting original equation assessment:** Frankenfield et al., *Analysis of Estimation Methods for Resting Metabolic Rate in Critically Ill Adults*, *Journal of Parenteral and Enteral Nutrition* 33(1) (2009), Methods and Results. The study reports standardized indirect calorimetry in 202 ventilated adult critical-care patients and an accuracy rate of 67% for the Penn State equation. [Publisher record](https://aspenjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0148607108322399)

**Model constraint:** Penn State predicts REE in kcal/day; it does not directly predict VCO₂. Conversion from REE to VCO₂ requires additional measured respiratory-gas information or an explicitly selected RQ. No RQ may be silently assumed.

### 5. Q2 modelling constraints established by the evidence

- Patients, staff and visitors must retain separate CO₂-generation classes.
- Persily Table 4 values are group estimates and must not be represented as patient-specific measurements.
- ICU cohort means must not be converted into one universal patient value.
- A mechanically ventilated patient's measured metabolic VCO₂ becomes a room-air source only if the expiratory-gas path releases it into the room. The ventilator exhaust/scavenging configuration is therefore a required site input.
- A single room-CO₂ observation cannot independently identify patient, staff and visitor counts unless some class counts, source strengths or event timings are known independently.

## Q11 — Size distributions

### 1. Kim viable-aerosol size structure

**Document:** Kim, Kim and Kim, *Distribution Characteristics of Airborne Bacteria and Fungi in the General Hospitals of Korea*  
**Publication/year:** *Industrial Health* 48(2) (2010), 236–243  
**DOI:** 10.2486/indhealth.48.236  
**Locators:** Materials and Methods, “Measurement,” p. 237; Results, “Size distribution of airborne bacteria and fungi in general hospital”; Figures 1–2; Tables 2–3  
**Primary article:** [J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/indhealth/48/2/48_2_236/_article)

The source reports the following six-stage Andersen cascade-impactor ranges:

| Andersen stage | Aerodynamic diameter reported by source |
|---:|---:|
| Stage 1 | >7.0 µm |
| Stage 2 | 4.7–7.0 µm |
| Stage 3 | 3.3–4.7 µm |
| Stage 4 | 2.1–3.3 µm |
| Stage 5 | 1.1–2.1 µm |
| Stage 6 | 0.65–1.1 µm |

Kim defines respirable concentration as the sum of stages 3–6. The study's sampler does not report a bin below 0.65 µm.

The model retains these six source-native ranges. It stores them in ascending-diameter order for computation, but this reordering does not alter a boundary or merge/re-bin the source data. The computational ordering is a model mapping, not a measured quantity.

### 2. Source-reported ICU viable totals

**Document/year:** Kim et al. (2010)  
**Locators:** Table 2 for bacteria; Table 3 for fungi

| Viable aerosol | ICU total | ICU respirable |
|---|---:|---:|
| Airborne bacteria | 202 CFU/m³ | 142 CFU/m³ |
| Airborne fungi | 65 CFU/m³ | 47 CFU/m³ |

The stage-specific distributions are presented graphically in Figures 1–2. Values digitized or estimated from those graphics must not be described as exact tabulated measurements.

The Stage 1 interval is open-ended (>7.0 µm). A midpoint, representative diameter or upper truncation would be a numerical modelling choice and is not reported by Kim.

### 3. Evidence required to close Q19

**Actual chiller COP — BLOCKED.** Required evidence:

- Manufacturer and exact model
- Chiller/compressor and condenser type
- Rated cooling capacity and refrigerant
- Entering/leaving chilled-water conditions
- Outdoor dry-bulb/wet-bulb or condenser-water conditions
- Certified full-load input and COP
- Certified IPLV/NPLV or part-load performance map

**Fan total electrical efficiency — BLOCKED.** Required evidence:

- Manufacturer and exact fan model/type
- Airflow at the selected duty point
- Total and/or static pressure at that duty point
- Fan total/static efficiency or shaft power
- Motor model, output, load fraction and numerical efficiency at load
- VFD and transmission efficiency, or directly measured total electrical input

Code minimums, IE classes and another product's performance curve cannot substitute for actual equipment data.
